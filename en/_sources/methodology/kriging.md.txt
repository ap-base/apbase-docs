# Ordinary kriging

`Kriging` implements *local ordinary kriging*: for each target point, a
linear combination of nearby source values that is the **best linear
unbiased estimator** (BLUE) under the spatial correlation structure
described by the fitted {doc}`variogram <variogram-fitting>`
{cite}`Matheron1971,Cressie1993,Isaaks1989`.

## Setup

Given $n$ source points with values $z_1, \dots, z_n$, the estimate at a
target $x_0$ is a weighted sum

$$
\hat{z}(x_0) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i \, z_i
$$

The weights $\lambda_i$ are chosen so that the estimator is:

- **Unbiased**: $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i = 1$. ("Ordinary" kriging means
  the underlying process mean is treated as unknown and constant within
  the local neighborhood, rather than assumed known -- this constraint
  is what makes the mean drop out of the estimator without needing to be
  supplied.)
- **Minimum variance**: among all weight sets satisfying the unbiasedness
  constraint, $\lambda_i$ minimizes the estimation variance
  $\operatorname{Var}[\hat{z}(x_0) - z(x_0)]$, using the semivariance
  $\gamma(h)$ between points at separation $h$ to quantify how
  (dis)similar values at that separation tend to be.

## The kriging system

Minimizing variance subject to a linear equality constraint is a
Lagrange-multiplier problem. Introducing multiplier $\mu$ for the
unbiasedness constraint and solving the resulting stationarity conditions
gives a linear system in the $n$ weights plus $\mu$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\gamma(d_{11}) & \cdots & \gamma(d_{1n}) & 1 \\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\gamma(d_{n1}) & \cdots & \gamma(d_{nn}) & 1 \\
1 & \cdots & 1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 \\ \vdots \\ \lambda_n \\ \mu \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \gamma(d_{10}) \\ \vdots \\ \gamma(d_{n0}) \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

where $d_{ij}$ is the distance between source points $i$ and $j$, and
$d_{i0}$ is the distance from source point $i$ to the target $x_0$. The
$(n+1) \times (n+1)$ matrix on the left is symmetric except for the
constraint row/column; the bottom row and right column (all ones, with a
zero corner) are exactly what enforces $\sum \lambda_i = 1$.

Solving this system gives both the kriging weights $\lambda_i$ and the
**kriging variance** -- an estimate of the uncertainty at $x_0$:

$$
\sigma_K^2(x_0) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i \, \gamma(d_{i0}) + \mu
$$

Unlike IDW's fixed distance-decay weights, kriging weights account for
redundancy between clustered source points (two nearby points that are
themselves close together contribute less independent information than
two points spread apart at the same average distance from the target).

## Local kriging

Solving the full system over every source point would scale poorly with
dataset size. `Kriging` instead builds and solves this system per target
using only the nearest `max_neighbors` source points within `radius` --
the same neighborhood construction used by `IDW`. `min_neighbors` sets a
floor below which a target returns `NaN` rather than a poorly-constrained
estimate.

## Supplying a variogram directly

`model_values` lets you pass a precomputed variogram model instead of
letting `fit` estimate one from `x`/`y`/`z` -- useful when comparing
multiple interpolation runs that should share exactly one fitted model
(this is what `apbase.cross_validate` does internally, fitting one
variogram and reusing it for both the IDW and kriging evaluation).
